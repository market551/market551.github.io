Commodities

Unlike financial securities that represent legal claims on paper, commodities are physical atoms: crude oil in pipelines, wheat in grain elevators, copper ore in smelters, and cattle on feedlots.

Fast Structural Summary

What is a Commodity Market at its Mechanical Core?

A commodity market coordinates the extraction, transformation, storage, and cross-continental transportation of fungible raw materials. Because natural assets have inelastic production timelines and perishable shelf lives, prices adjust violently to clear momentary physical supply bottlenecks.

2. The Five Pillars of Commodity Price Formation

1. Inelastic Supply in the Short Run

You cannot build a copper mine or drill a deepwater oil field in 48 hours. When demand spikes unexpectedly, supply cannot immediately expand. Price must spike to rationing levels.

2. Storage Costs & Carry Economics

Storing 100,000 bushels of corn costs money (silo leasing, ventilation, fumigation, insurance). When storage is abundant, future prices trade above spot (Contango). When inventories run out, spot prices trade at massive premiums (Backwardation).

3. Weather & Climate Disruption

Droughts, frosts, and monsoon anomalies directly destroy agricultural yields. A single freeze in Florida or Brazil can instantly eliminate 20% of global orange juice or coffee supply.

4. Geopolitical Chokepoints

Over 20% of global petroleum passes through the Strait of Hormuz. When maritime straits, pipelines, or sovereign export ports face military blockade or sanctions, global prices reprice overnight.

3. The Negative Price Event (April 20, 2020)

On April 20, 2020, the May contract for WTI Crude Oil closed at -$37.63 per barrel. How could a valuable energy commodity have a negative price?

The Mechanics of Storage Inelasticity

  1. Global lockdowns eliminated transportation fuel demand while oil producers continued pumping barrels.
  2. Storage tanks at the physical delivery hub (Cushing, Oklahoma) filled to 100% capacity.
  3. Financial traders holding expiring May futures contracts faced mandatory physical delivery: taking actual train-cars of oil in Oklahoma.
  4. Because traders had zero access to storage tanks or pipelines, they were forced to pay buyers $37 per barrel simply to take the physical oil off their hands and avoid catastrophic environmental penalties.
Scientific Classification of Commodity Data
FACT

Commodity futures contracts specify precise legal physical standards: sulfur content for oil (sweet vs sour), test weight for grains, and purity percentage for bullion.

DATA

The US Energy Information Administration (EIA) publishes weekly petroleum status reports detailing crude inventories in millions of barrels.

INTERPRETATION

A drop in crude inventories is interpreted by markets as tight physical supply, though it may also reflect temporary seasonal refinery turnaround schedules.

HYPOTHESIS

The Hotelling Rule hypothesizes that non-renewable resource prices should rise at the nominal interest rate over centuries as reserves deplete. Empirical verification remains disputed.
