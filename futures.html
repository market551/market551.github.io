Futures

A futures contract is an exchange-traded, legally binding commitment to purchase or sell a specified quantity and grade of an underlying asset at an agreed price, with delivery or cash settlement on a predetermined future date.

Fast Structural Summary

Why Do Futures Exist at Their Mechanical Core?

Futures exist to eliminate forward price uncertainty for commercial producers and consumers. By standardizing contract terms and routing trades through a Central Clearinghouse (CCP) with daily mark-to-market margin requirements, futures eliminate counterparty default risk and concentrate global liquidity.

1. The Central Counterparty (CCP) Revolution

In an unstandardized over-the-counter forward contract, two private parties promise to trade in 6 months. If one party goes bankrupt, the other faces complete counterparty default. Futures solve this by placing the Clearinghouse between every trade:

2. Margin Mechanics & Daily Mark-to-Market

In a futures contract, no purchase price is paid upfront. Instead, both buyer and seller post collateral:

Initial Margin

A performance bond (typically 3%–12% of the contract's total notional value) deposited with the clearing broker before an order can be executed.

Daily Mark-to-Market

At the close of every trading day, every open contract is repriced to the official settlement price. Losses are debited in real cash from the losing account and immediately credited to the winning account.

Maintenance Margin & Margin Calls

If accumulated losses cause the collateral balance to drop below the maintenance threshold, the participant receives an urgent margin call to wire additional funds immediately, or their position is liquidated into the market.

3. The Symbiosis: Commercial Hedgers vs. Financial Speculators

Role Commercial Hedger Financial Speculator
Primary Incentive Lock in profit margins and eliminate price risk in their physical business. Absorb price risk in pursuit of capital gain.
Real World Example A commercial wheat farmer selling December futures in June to guarantee a $6/bushel selling price before harvest. A proprietary trading firm taking the opposite side of the farmer's order.
Underlying Asset Exposure Already holds an opposite exposure in the physical world (owns the wheat field). Has zero interest in physical grain; closes the position prior to delivery expiration.
Economic Contribution Stabilizes real food, fuel, and raw material supply planning. Provides the resting liquidity that allows hedgers to execute without massive price slippage.

4. Settlement: Physical Delivery vs. Cash Settlement

Every futures contract terminates at its expiration date through one of two mechanisms:

1. Physical Delivery

The short position delivers actual warehouse receipts or pipeline pump notices to the long position at designated exchange facilities (e.g., CME corn or NYMEX crude). Over 98% of financial participants close out their contracts before the "First Notice Day" to avoid taking physical delivery.

2. Cash Settlement

For assets that cannot be physically delivered (such as the S&P 500 stock index or SOFR interest rate benchmarks), the final settlement is purely an electronic wire transfer of the net cash difference between the trade price and final index value.

Scientific Classification of Futures Market Architecture
FACT

Futures contracts are zero-sum contracts prior to trading fees: for every dollar gained on a long position, exactly one dollar is lost by a short position.

DATA

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) publishes the weekly Commitments of Traders (COT) report classifying open interest by commercial hedgers vs non-commercial speculators.

INTERPRETATION

Extreme speculative positioning in COT reports is often interpreted by market technicians as a contrarian indicator of sentiment exhaustion.

HYPOTHESIS

Keynes' theory of "Normal Backwardation" hypothesized that hedgers must offer speculators an expected risk premium, causing futures prices to systematically trade below expected future spot prices. Modern empirical evidence shows mixed support.
