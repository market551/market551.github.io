The Seven Global Market Systems

A financial market is not a single amorphous casino. It is a specialized network of institutions, legal contracts, clearinghouses, and participants designed to allocate capital and transfer specific categories of economic risk.

Below is the master taxonomy. Each market answers the seven fundamental structural questions: What is it, why does it exist, what is traded, who participates, how price forms, what moves it, and how it developed.

MARKET SYSTEM 01

Foreign Exchange (Forex)

What is it?

The global decentralized network for exchanging one national currency for another in exchange pairs (e.g., EUR/USD, USD/JPY).

Why does it exist?

Every sovereign country maintains its own legal tender. Without an exchange mechanism, cross-border commerce and international investment would be impossible.

What is traded?

Claims on commercial bank deposits denominated in specific sovereign monetary units.

Who participates?

Central banks (setting interest rates and foreign reserves), global commercial banks (market making), corporations (converting export earnings), and asset managers.

How does price form?

Continuous electronic matching through interbank electronic communication networks (EBS, Refinitiv) driven by continuous order flow.

What moves it & How it developed?

Moved by relative interest rate differentials, inflation rates, and geopolitical shifts. Evolved from gold-backed bullion standards through Bretton Woods (1944) to the floating fiat exchange rates initiated in 1971.

MARKET SYSTEM 02

Commodities

What is it?

Markets for raw physical goods categorized into energy (oil, gas), agriculture (grains, livestock), and metals (industrial copper, precious gold).

Why does it exist?

Natural resources are geographically concentrated while human consumption is global. Producers need forward cash flow to invest in multi-year extraction and crops.

What is traded?

Standardized physical grades (e.g., Brent Crude, #2 Yellow Corn, Grade A Copper Cathode) meeting strict delivery specs.

Who participates?

Physical producers (miners, agribusinesses), commercial consumers (airlines, power plants, food processors), and physical commodity trading houses (Trafigura, Glencore).

How does price form?

Through the interaction of prompt physical spot transactions, pipeline/shipping freight constraints, and terminal storage inventory levels.

What moves it & How it developed?

Weather events, maritime choke-points, extraction depletion, and OPEC quota agreements. Developed from ancient port bazaars to formal exchanges like the Chicago Board of Trade (1848) and London Metal Exchange (1877).

MARKET SYSTEM 03

Equities (Stocks)

What is it?

Public secondary marketplaces where investors buy and sell fractional ownership certificates in joint-stock corporations.

Why does it exist?

To separate ownership from daily management, pool massive societal savings for high-scale enterprise, and provide original investors an exit mechanism without liquidating factories.

What is traded?

Common and preferred equity shares conferring proportional voting rights and residual claims on corporate net earnings after debt service.

Who participates?

Issuing companies (in primary IPOs), pension and mutual funds, designated market makers (DMMs), quantitative high-frequency market participants, and individual retail investors.

How does price form?

Central limit order books (CLOB) where continuous bids and asks clear against the national best bid and offer (NBBO).

What moves it & How it developed?

Company earnings reports, discount rate expectations, economic demand cycles, and competitive moats. Formed in Amsterdam (1602) to finance spice voyagers through shared risk.

MARKET SYSTEM 04

Fixed Income (Bonds)

What is it?

The debt capital market where entities raise money by issuing formal legal promises to pay specified interest (coupons) and return principal at maturity.

Why does it exist?

Governments and corporations require non-dilutive, predictable capital to finance infrastructure, wars, dams, and long-term industrial projects.

What is traded?

Sovereign treasuries, municipal revenue bonds, corporate debt obligations, and securitized mortgage pools.

Who participates?

Finance ministries, primary dealer investment banks, insurance balance sheets matching long-term liabilities, and commercial banks.

How does price form?

Inverse price-yield arithmetic: when prevailing market interest rates rise, existing fixed coupon bonds fall in dollar price so their effective yield matches the new benchmark.

What moves it & How it developed?

Central bank base rates, inflation expectations, and sovereign default risk spreads. Evolved from Venetian Renaissance prestiti bonds (12th century) used to finance naval armadas.

MARKET SYSTEM 05

Futures

What is it?

Exchange-traded derivative contracts mandating the purchase or sale of a specified asset at a predetermined price on a specific future calendar date.

Why does it exist?

To eliminate counterparty default risk in forwards and allow physical producers and commercial buyers to lock in prices months or years before harvest or delivery.

What is traded?

Strictly standardized contracts covering agricultural goods, energy, interest rate benchmarks (SOFR), equity indexes (S&P 500 E-mini), and FX.

Who participates?

Commercial hedgers (producers seeking price protection), financial speculators providing opposite liquidity, and central clearing brokers.

How does price form?

Through the cost-of-carry model: Spot Price + Financing Interest Cost + Storage/Insurance Cost − Convenience Yield.

What moves it & How it developed?

Spot price fluctuations, storage tank capacity, interest rates, and calendar roll dynamics. Born in 18th-century Dojima Rice Exchange (Osaka, Japan) and 19th-century Chicago.

MARKET SYSTEM 06

Options

What is it?

Conditional contracts granting the holder the legal right—without the legal obligation—to buy (Call) or sell (Put) an underlying asset at a fixed Strike Price on or before expiration.

Why does it exist?

To create financial insurance: allowing market participants to cap worst-case losses while preserving unlimited upside participation, and to price volatility itself.

What is traded?

Standardized call and put contracts with discrete strike intervals and expiration calendar cycles.

Who participates?

Institutional asset managers hedging portfolio drawdowns, automated options market makers maintaining delta-neutral books, and retail traders.

How does price form?

Determined by Black-Scholes-Merton pricing mechanics: Underlying spot price, Strike price, Time to expiration, Risk-free interest rate, and Implied Volatility.

What moves it & How it developed?

Changes in implied volatility expectations (IV crush/expansion), time decay (theta), and underlying asset momentum. Traced back to Thales of Miletus securing olive presses, formalized at the Chicago Board Options Exchange (CBOE) in 1973.

MARKET SYSTEM 07

Cryptographic Assets

What is it?

A public digital ledger market where tokens and state execution rights exist across decentralized cryptographic consensus networks without centralized clearinghouses.

Why does it exist?

To eliminate single points of failure in settlement, enable 24/7 borderless transfer of digital value, and provide programmable smart contracts without intermediaries.

What is traded?

Protocol base currencies (e.g., BTC, ETH), fiat-pegged stablecoins (USDT, USDC), governance tokens, and on-chain debt claims.

Who participates?

Centralized digital exchanges (CEXs), decentralized automated market makers (AMMs), quantitative arbitrageurs, protocol foundations, and global retail participants.

How does price form?

Split between off-chain central limit order books (Binance, Coinbase) and on-chain liquidity pool invariant equations (e.g., x × y = k), connected via high-speed cross-venue arbitrage.

What moves it & How it developed?

Global dollar liquidity cycles, regulatory enforcement actions, protocol upgrades, and stablecoin inflows. Emerged in 2008 with Satoshi Nakamoto's Bitcoin whitepaper following the Global Financial Crisis.
