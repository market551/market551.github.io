Equities (Stocks)

A share of stock is not a lottery ticket or an abstract chart ticker. It is a fractional, legally enforceable ownership claim on the residual assets and future cash flows of a corporation.

Fast Structural Summary

What is a Stock at its Mechanical Core?

A stock represents a unit of equity capital in a limited liability company. Shareholders are the "residual claimants": after a corporation pays suppliers, employees, taxes, and debt interest, whatever net cash remains belongs proportionally to the shareholders as dividends or retained earnings.

2. What Determines Stock Price: The Discounted Cash Flow Engine

A stock is priced not on what happened last year, but on future expected cash flows discounted back to the present day using the prevailing cost of capital (interest rates plus an equity risk premium):

Intrinsic Value = ∑ [ Expected Free Cash Flowt / (1 + r)t ]

Where r is the discount rate (Risk-Free Sovereign Rate + Equity Risk Premium). Notice the arithmetic consequence: when central banks raise interest rates, the denominator (1 + r) increases, automatically lowering the present discounted value of all future corporate earnings!

3. The Expectations Trap: Why Good Earnings Can Cause a Drop

New investors are often shocked when a company reports record-breaking profit growth of +25%, yet the stock drops -8% at the market open.

1. The Baseline Price Already Priced In Perfection

Stock prices reflect aggregate expectations. If institutional analysts already anticipated +30% growth, a +25% result is an objective disappointment relative to what was baked into the share price.

2. Forward Guidance Dominates Past Performance

Past earnings cannot be changed. What drives immediate order flow is the Chief Executive's guidance for the next four quarters. If future margins are forecasted to contract, current stock value drops.

3. Multiple Compression

If bond yields rise dramatically, investors demand a higher yield on equities. Even if earnings are stable, the Price-to-Earnings (P/E) multiple compresses, dragging down the share price.

Scientific Classification of Equity Markets
FACT

In corporate liquidation bankruptcy, common equity holders are strictly last in the priority of claims behind secured debt, bondholders, and preferred stock.

DATA

Long-term historical annualized real returns for the S&P 500 index have averaged approximately 6.5% to 7.0% after inflation over the past century (Source: Ibbotson/Siegel).

INTERPRETATION

The historical equity premium is interpreted by financial economists as compensation for enduring terrifying 40%–50% peak-to-trough drawdowns during severe recessions.

HYPOTHESIS

The Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH) asserts that stock prices always fully reflect all available information. Behavioral anomalies and speculative bubbles suggest limitations.
